# Forrest Frank Gains 1.2M Followers in 35 Days

**How one fan vote changed the trajectory of his career — and generated 15M+ Spotify streams.**

---

## The Setup

Instead of keeping his creative process private, Forrest Frank gave his fans a front-row seat. He uploaded five unreleased demos into The Tree House — his Grouped community — and let members vote on which one he should finish and release.

Fans overwhelmingly chose a track called "Good Day." The catch: Forrest, his wife, and his collaborators all agreed it was probably too simple and wouldn't go anywhere. But Forrest trusted the vote. He finished the song and released it.

---

## The Result

What followed was the most explosive 60 days of Forrest Frank's career:

- **1.2 million new Instagram followers**
- **15+ million streams on Spotify alone**
- **Sold-out arena shows as a headliner**

None of it would have happened without that vote.

---

## Quote

> "Never would have released if it wasn't for the vote. Now it's my most-streamed song."
> — Forrest Frank

---

## How It Worked

**The Group as a Focus Group**
Forrest used Grouped's voting and polling functionality to involve fans directly in the creative process before any public release. Fans didn't just consume — they contributed. The result was a decision no label A&R would have greenlit, made possible only because the artist had a direct, owned channel to his most invested listeners.

**Premiere Access**
The demos were shared exclusively inside the Group before anything went public. Fans got first access — not as a marketing gimmick, but as a genuine invitation into the creative process. The Group is where fans got everything first.

**Release Compounding**
Because Forrest already had an owned audience in The Tree House, the moment "Good Day" dropped publicly it had a pre-built group of invested fans ready to stream, share, and amplify it. The 1.2M Instagram follower growth was a downstream effect of owning the relationship before the release.

---

## Key Takeaway

Fans who vote are fans who invest. Giving fans creative ownership created an army of advocates before the song was even public. The platform didn't do the work — the community did.
